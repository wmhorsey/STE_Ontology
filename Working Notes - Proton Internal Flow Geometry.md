## 1. The Problem

The main ontology (Section 10.7) defines the quark chain topology as **UDU** (proton) and **DUD** (neutron) — an alternating sequence of voids and concentrations. But it never specifies:

- The **3D spatial arrangement** of the three quarks within the baryon volume
- Whether each up quark (micro-BH) develops **rotational axis structure** (disk-plane + jet axis) at nuclear scale
- What the **relative orientation** of two up quarks' rotation axes is
- How the down quark's outward-dominant radiation geometry interacts with the up quarks' inward-dominant accretion geometry
- What **shape** the void shells take under internal baryon flow stress

The chain topology is *topological* (which quarks share flow boundaries), not *geometric* (they are not necessarily in a line). This distinction matters.

## 2. Core Proposal: Triangular Arrangement

### 2.1 The Three Quarks Form a Triangle

The down quark is **not** physically sandwiched between the two up quarks. It sits **off to one side**, forming a triangle:

```
        U₁
       / \
      /   \
     /     \
    D ───── U₂
```

- U₁ and U₂ occupy two vertices (micro-void cores with shells)
- D occupies the third vertex (saturated STE concentration)
- Each quark's sphere of influence is ~0.29 fm (~1/3 baryon volume), per Section 10.2.1
- The insulation between U₁ and U₂ comes from D's **radiation field filling the gap**, not from D's body physically sitting between them
- The UDU "chain" describes flow connectivity (D connects to both U's), not linear geometry

### 2.2 Why a Triangle?

A linear arrangement (U-D-U in a line) would:
- Put all three quarks' strongest flow channels along one axis — a fragile geometry
- Create a prolate (elongated) baryon shape inconsistent with the proton's roughly spherical charge distribution
- Leave two faces of the baryon with no quark occupation — wasted volume

A triangle:
- Distributes quarks across the baryon volume more evenly
- Creates a stable three-point support structure
- Allows each quark's flow channels to point in different directions, covering more solid angle
- Is consistent with deep inelastic scattering data showing three distinct scattering centers that are not collinear

## 3. Up Quark Rotation Axes and Offset Alignment

### 3.1 Each Up Quark Has Axis Structure

If scale invariance holds (Section 12), each up quark micro-BH has rotation and therefore:
- A **rotation axis** (jet axis)
- A **disk plane** (accretion/equatorial plane perpendicular to the jet axis)
- Preferential inflow along the disk plane
- Preferential outflow (radiation) along the jet axis

This is the same structure seen in astrophysical black holes, at nuclear scale.

### 3.2 The Axes Are Offset ~45–60°

The two up quarks' rotation axes are **not parallel, not perpendicular, but offset by approximately 45–60 degrees**. The specific angle is determined by **charge interaction energy minimization**:

- Each quark's charge is produced at specific surface regions (chirality-directed mismatch, per Section 10.2.1a)
- Two +2/3 charge regions (U₁, U₂) repel each other
- One -1/3 charge region (D) attracts both
- The equilibrium angle minimizes total interaction energy: reduces U-U repulsion (charge-producing faces rotated partially away from each other) while maintaining D-U attraction (D's radiating face aimed at the convergence zone)

### 3.3 What This Creates Inside the Baryon

The offset axes + triangular arrangement creates:

- A **convergence zone** near the center of the triangle where both up quarks' accretion flows partially overlap
- **Multiple distinct outflow channels** — each U's jets escape the baryon at different angles, plus D's radial output exits from its vertex
- **Choke barriers** at the intersection of the two accretion flow planes, especially at the triangle center — these are the "geometric barriers" referenced in Section 10.2.2 that implement confinement
- An asymmetric, complex internal flow pattern that redistributes angular momentum into orbital channels between the quarks

## 4. Internal Circulation: Bidirectional Feeding

### 4.1 All Three Quarks Both Attract and Radiate

Per Section 10.2.1, both up and down quarks attract and radiate. The internal loop is bidirectional:

- **U shells radiate outward** → some radiation flows to D, sustaining its saturation shell and core
- **D radiates outward from its vertex** → some radiation flows into U accretion zones, feeding their inflow
- **U jets + D radial output** escape the baryon through separate channels at different angles

The three quarks form a **closed circulation system** where energy cycles between them. The voids are content — they do their thing permanently. The down quark stays saturated because the up quarks feed its shell.

### 4.2 Measured Charge = Surface Leakage

What we measure as quark charge (2/3, 1/3) and quark energy is the **surface leakage** — the net mismatch between what each quark pulls vs. radiates that *isn't absorbed by the other quarks*. The internal circulation is a closed loop; what leaks out is a small fraction of the total flow.

This is why protons are stable forever — the voids are "right at home." They aren't fighting anything. The internal circulation sustains itself with no external input. The down quark's core is saturated (can't absorb more), so it radiates, and that radiation is the system's internal energy transport mechanism.

## 5. Insulation in Triangular Geometry

The down quark must prevent U₁ and U₂ from merging via the soap-bubble rule (Section 8.6). In a triangular arrangement, D is not physically between U₁ and U₂. The insulation works through:

1. **D's radiation field**: D radiates outward from its vertex. This radiation fills the space between U₁ and U₂, creating an energy-dense barrier between the two voids.

2. **Flow-plane intersection**: Each U's disk-plane accretion creates flow in a different plane (because the axes are offset). The intersection of these two flow planes at the triangle center creates a turbulent choke zone — a region of such high flow complexity that void-void sightline contact is impossible.

3. **Energy density in the gap**: The U₁-U₂ gap is the highest-energy region in the baryon — both U's are pulling from that direction AND D is pushing into it. This creates a saturated zone that acts as a physical barrier.

The insulation is **field-based**, not body-based. D doesn't need to be between U₁ and U₂ — its radiation and the combined flow geometry do the blocking.

## 6. Predictions and Connections

### 6.1 Proton Anomalous Magnetic Moment

Orthodox prediction for a simple spin-1/2 particle: 1.0 nuclear magneton.
Measured value: 2.793 nuclear magnetons.

Two tilted rotation axes + an off-center radiator produces a complex magnetic structure, not a simple dipole. The offset angle and triangular arrangement should predict a magnetic moment significantly different from 1.0 — possible path to deriving 2.793.

### 6.2 Proton Spin Puzzle

Only ~30% of the proton's spin comes from quark spins. If the two up quarks' axes are offset, a significant fraction of the total angular momentum is stored in **orbital flow** between the quarks — in the circulation channels, not in individual quark rotations. The tilted geometry redistributes angular momentum.

### 6.3 Proton Charge Radius

The triangular arrangement with a specific offset angle predicts a specific effective charge distribution profile. The charge radius (~0.84 fm) should emerge from the triangle geometry + the leakage fraction calculation.

### 6.4 Neutron Comparison

The neutron (DUD) would have a different triangle — two concentrations and one void. The D-D pair might sit closer together (both outward-dominant, so less repulsion between them), with the single U offset. The different triangle geometry + single-axis rotation should predict the neutron's magnetic moment (-1.913 nuclear magnetons — negative, and different magnitude from the proton).

## 7. Shape Deformation of Up Quark Shells

The document describes void-core shells as spherical. Under internal baryon stress, the up quark shells may deform. The question is how much and in what direction.

### What We Know
- The up quarks are embedded in an extreme flow environment (inside a baryon, not free space)
- Each U is pulled toward the triangle center by D's attraction and the other U's attraction
- Each U's jets are partially blocked by the other quarks' flow channels

### Possibilities
- **Oblate**: Squashed along the axis pointing toward the triangle center. The internal baryon flow pressure is strongest from the center, compressing the shells along that direction. Similar to how astrophysical BH ergospheres are oblate from spin.
- **Toroidal**: In extreme cases, the oblate deformation goes so far that the shell becomes donut-shaped. This would mean the void itself has a toroidal topology. Unclear if the energy conditions support this at nuclear scale.
- **Irregularly deformed**: Asymmetric stress from the triangular arrangement might produce non-axisymmetric deformation. The side facing D gets different stress than the side facing U₂.

**Current intuition**: Probably more extreme than simple oblate, but unclear if full torus. May depend on local flow density level. This needs simulation.

## 8. The "White Hole" Question

The down quark is outward-dominant (radiation > attraction). This is functionally the topological inverse of the up quark's micro-black-hole (inward-dominant, attraction > radiation). Calling it a "white hole" is suggestive:

- White holes: outward-dominant, radiation source
- Black holes: inward-dominant, attraction sink
- The baryon contains both — a micro black hole/white hole system in circulation

**Not formalizing this yet.** The label is evocative but may be misleading — the down quark has no void, no shell, and is not self-sustaining. It's a saturated concentration, not a reversed cavitation event. The physics is different even if the flow direction is inverted. Recording the idea for later evaluation.

## 10. Relevance of Iseki, Sasaki & Shiozawa (2025) — "Quantum Electron Clouds near Black Holes: Black Atoms and Molecules"

**Paper**: arXiv:2511.11173v2, published in Phys. Rev. D 113 (2026) 025015.

### 10.1 What the Paper Does

This paper studies what happens when you solve the Schrödinger equation near black holes using DeWitt's curved-space quantization formalism. They examine:

1. **Free particles near a Schwarzschild BH**: Wave functions get attracted toward the horizon. Higher energy = more attraction. Probability density diverges at the horizon.

2. **"Black hydrogen atom" (Schwarzschild + potential)**: An electron bound to a Schwarzschild BH via an attractive potential. The electron cloud localizes near the horizon in a thin shell. Quantum effects (uncertainty principle) push the peak slightly outward from the horizon surface — but gravity wins when the horizon is large enough.

3. **Reissner-Nordström "black hydrogen"**: A genuinely charged BH trapping an electron via its own electric field. Key finding: **gravitational repulsion near the inner (Cauchy) horizon** pushes the wave function outward from the core. Wave functions attach to the *outer* horizon from outside and to the *inner* horizon from inside. New orbital peaks appear near the horizon that don't exist in flat-space hydrogen.

4. **Extremal limit**: As charge increases toward extremality, the wave function forms a **shell of high probability density at the degenerate horizon** — electron cloud wraps the horizon from both sides.

5. **"Black hydrogen molecule" (Majumdar-Papapetrou binary)**: Two extremal charged BHs + one shared electron, analogous to H₂⁺ molecular ion. The BHs are stable because gravitational attraction and electromagnetic repulsion balance. The equation is solved in spheroidal coordinates. Key finding: the binary BH behaves like a molecular nucleus — the electron is shared between the two BH centers.

### 10.2 What's Useful for STE

Several findings align with or inform the STE ontology in interesting ways:

#### A. Electron Clouds Localize at Shells — Not at Centers

The paper repeatedly shows that electrons don't fall into the BH. Instead, they **localize at the horizon (shell)** in a thin, high-probability-density layer. This is exactly what the STE model predicts: the shell is the physical surface, the void interior is empty, and structure forms *at* the shell boundary. Orthodox physics gets the same result via uncertainty principle vs. gravitational attraction, but the STE interpretation is cleaner — there's nothing inside for the electron to fall into, so of course it parks at the shell.

**STE translation**: The electron wave function localizing at the horizon IS the electron parking at the shell. Same result, different ontological explanation.

#### B. Gravitational Repulsion Inside Charged Black Holes

The Reissner-Nordström BH shows gravitational repulsion near the inner horizon and core (from the $Q^2/r^2$ term dominating $-a/r$ at small $r$). In STE terms, the RN metric's "charge" maps to the flow mismatch at the void shell. The repulsive core behavior might correspond to the outward-dominant radiation zone of a structure with significant charge (flow mismatch).

**Potentially relevant to the down quark question**: If a charged void-core has a repulsive zone near its center, and the down quark is the "outward-dominant" partner, then the down quark's behavior might be the *limiting case* of this repulsive interior — the point where the outward (repulsive) term completely dominates the inward (attractive) term, and you get a structure that is fully outward-dominant. This is speculative but worth tracking.

#### C. New Orbital Peaks Near the Horizon

In the RN case, the wave function develops additional peaks near the outer horizon that don't exist in flat-space hydrogen. The paper says this means "more stable orbits" and "different chemical properties."

**STE translation**: This maps directly to Section 4.4.1's compression gradient zones. Near a void-core shell, the compression is extreme, and the STE ontology predicts additional resonance radii (orbital shells) that don't appear in the ambient-field case. The paper's extra peaks ARE these additional compression-gradient orbital shells, derived from different math but pointing at the same physics.

#### D. The Black Molecule — Binary BH as Molecular Nucleus

Section 5 of the paper treats two extremal charged BHs sharing one electron, using Majumdar-Papapetrou (MP) geometry. The MP BHs are stable because gravity and EM repulsion balance.

**This directly parallels the proton's UDU structure**: Two micro-void-cores (up quarks) sharing a flow environment, with a concentration (down quark) mediating between them. The paper's "black molecule" is the cosmological-scale analog of the baryon's internal structure — two void cores + shared electron cloud.

Key detail: In the MP solution, the binary BHs sit at fixed positions because gravitational attraction and electromagnetic repulsion *exactly cancel*. The paper solves the electron wave function in this fixed geometry.

**STE question**: Does the proton's UDU triangle work the same way? Are U₁ and U₂ at fixed separation because their gravitational (void-shell-to-shell) attraction is balanced by their charge (+2/3 vs +2/3) repulsion? If so, the offset angle and triangle size might be derivable from the same balance condition that defines MP BHs, just at nuclear scale.

#### E. Spheroidal Coordinates and Variable Separation

The paper uses spheroidal coordinates (ξ, η, φ) for the binary BH molecule. In flat space, the H₂⁺ equation separates cleanly in these coordinates. In the curved BH case, the curvature terms break the separation — the ξ and η sectors couple.

**STE implication**: If the proton's internal geometry is a binary micro-BH system, then the natural coordinate system for describing its internal structure is **spheroidal**, not spherical. The fact that curvature breaks the variable separation in the paper's equations suggests that the proton's internal flow equations are inherently coupled in ξ and η — which would mean the radial and angular structure of the proton cannot be treated independently. This might be relevant to why the proton's internal structure is so much richer than a simple three-point model suggests.

#### F. Horizon-Scale Black Holes Exist in the Current Universe

The paper estimates that a BH with Schwarzschild radius equal to the Bohr radius (~10⁻¹⁰ m) has mass ~6.7×10¹⁶ kg (small asteroid mass) and Hawking evaporation time ~10²⁴ years (far longer than the age of the universe). These primordial BHs could exist today and could trap electrons to form "black atoms."

**STE note**: This is interesting but operates at a completely different scale than the micro-BH up quarks (~10⁻¹⁵ m). The paper's "black atoms" are intermediate-scale objects — BH nuclei at atomic-orbital scale, not at quark scale. Still, the principle is the same: void-core + electron cloud = atom-like structure, at any scale where the numbers work.

### 10.3 What's NOT Useful (or Conflicts)

- The paper assumes GR spacetime curvature is real. STE says space doesn't curve; the effects attributed to curvature are density gradient effects. The math may transfer but the interpretation doesn't.
- The paper treats the Schrödinger equation as fundamental. STE would say the wave equation is emergent from collapse dynamics, not a starting axiom.
- The "gravitational repulsion" inside RN BHs is a metric effect in GR. In STE, the interior is void — there's nothing there to repel. The RN repulsive core might not have an STE analog at all, or it might map to something completely different (like the radiation pressure from the shell's inner face).
- The paper doesn't address void-core architecture. All its BHs have singularities (or naked singularities in the overextremal case). The STE void-core model would predict different near-horizon behavior than the paper's calculations show.

### 10.4 Bottom Line

The paper's most useful contribution to this working note is **Section 5 (the black molecule)**: it provides a rigorous framework for how two charged void-cores sharing an electron behave quantum mechanically. The balance condition (gravity = EM repulsion) that stabilizes the MP binary might be the same mechanism that sets the U₁-U₂ separation and offset angle in the proton. If the STE model can translate the MP balance condition into the nuclear-scale language of flow mismatch and void-shell coupling, it might provide a derivation path for the proton's internal geometry.

The electron-localization-at-shells result (Sections 3-4) is nice confirmation that the STE picture (structure forms at shells, not at centers) is consistent with orthodox quantum mechanics, even if the ontological interpretation differs.
